\abstract{Large language model (LLM) agents increasingly operate in open-world environments where security risks cannot be fully predefined. However, most existing guardrail mechanisms remain static, relying on fixed rules, prompts, or classifiers that generalize poorly to emerging threats. Here we present EVOLVE, a self-evolving security framework that continuously generates, reuses, audits, and refines executable guardrail tools through lifelong interaction. Inspired by the cumulative adaptive processes through which human systems develop resilience to novel risks, EVOLVE treats defense not as a fixed policy, but as an evolving security capability. Across multiple benchmarks, backbone models, and embodied environments, EVOLVE consistently outperforms existing defenses across diverse threat scenarios with +22\% average F1 score. We further validate the framework in real-world robotic and autonomous coding systems. Our results suggest that scalable agent safety may require continuously evolving defense systems rather than static guardrails.}
