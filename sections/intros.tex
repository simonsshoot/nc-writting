Building safe and reliable intelligent agents represents a core challenge in the pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)~\citep{fang2025comprehensivesurveyselfevolvingai}. In recent years, significant progress has been made in intelligent agents driven primarily by large language models (LLMs)~\citep{gao2025evovlesurvey}. These agents are capable of autonomously managing files, invoking tools, performing operations, and interacting with their environments, leading to their widespread application across various aspects of daily life~\citep{yu2025finmem,yang2024llmpsychology,gur2023webagent}. Nevertheless, the continuous expansion of their capabilities results in a broadening attack surface for agent security, which may lead to greater risks. For instance, a medical agent could misuse permissions to leak patient data~\citep{yuan2024rjudge}, while an autonomous driving agent might misinterpret user instructions and execute dangerous maneuvers~\citep{han2025dme,mao2023driving}. Establishing effective safety mechanisms for LLM-based agents has thus become a pressing imperative.

Although existing defense methods for intelligent agents have achieved some results, they generally exhibit limitations in generalization and inadequate protection when confronted with unknown and constantly evolving scenarios. Prompt-based methods, such as CoTSafe~\citep{yin2024safeagentbench}, which embed safety constraints in the reasoning chain to steer agents away from risks, suffer from insufficient reliability as they rely solely on the ambiguous judgments of natural language. Model-based methods~\citep{zhang2024asb} employ specifically trained discriminators to identify dangerous behaviors, yet their performance is highly dependent on the data distribution. Rule or knowledge-based methods~\citep{xiang2024guardagent,sadhu2024athena} monitor agent actions using predefined safety guidelines, which are inherently drawn from a priori human knowledge, making them difficult to scale in the face of emerging domains. These approaches collectively reveal a core contradiction: existing defense mechanisms are static, while real-world scenarios are dynamic\citep{shahriar2025survey}.

This contradiction prompts us to re-examine the essence of agent defense from a new perspective. When confronting complex and changing environments, humans possess a crucial capability: the ability to continually abstract and introspect from environmental interactions, thereby refining decision-making strategies and forming transferable, generalized coping mechanisms, rather than merely memorizing known threats. Such a capacity for "self-evolution" is precisely what static defense frameworks lack~\citep{cai2025building}. Thus, a natural question arises: \emph{Can we construct an agent defense framework centered on self-evolution, one capable of exhibiting adaptability in dynamically changing environments?}

For methods based on self-evolution, a significant challenge lies in ensuring evolution does not go astray~\citep{shao2025misevovle}. Agents face the risk of mis-evolution, and updates to the knowledge library require validation. Some works lack careful auditing of newly acquired knowledge, implicitly assuming that ``newer is better'', which is often the root cause of misevolution~\citep{shao2025misevovle,han2025alignment}. Our experiments show that even when safety constraints are incorporated into tool design, the guardrail tools evolved by an agent may still pose risks—a phenomenon we define as \emph{Vulnerabilities in Guardrail Tools (VGT)}. Safety is not a one-time attribute but a dynamic process that requires ongoing verification throughout evolution~\citep{shahriar2025survey}. Therefore, it is crucial to establish an independent auditing system capable of evaluating the new knowledge generated during self-evolution process.

We propose DEFEND framework (\underline{D}ynamic Self-\underline{E}volution \underline{f}ramework for \underline{En}hancing Agent’s \underline{D}efense Ability), the first to combine agent security with agent self-evolution. The core concept of DEFEND is to treat the defense mechanism itself as a dynamically generated, continuously evolving, and lifelong learning artifact (Python tools), rather than a pre-defined and fixed set of rules. DEFEND's core consists of four agent components. AnalysisAgent performs in-depth risk analysis on runtime user requests and agent operations by leveraging a lifelong risk knowledge library, and dynamically generates targeted, executable Python guardrail tools to achieve cross-domain defense generalization. FusionAgent retrieves semantically similar existing tools from the tool library and fuses them with new ones, enabling precise reuse and continuous evolution. ExecutorAgent safely executes protection tools and agent operations within a sandboxed environment, providing verifiable execution evidence. Finally, AuditorAgent independently audits the logical integrity and security of each evolved tool and synthesizes signals from all modules to make final security judgment, forming an autonomous closed loop from risk perception and tool evolution to trusted verification.

By being the first to integrate agent security with agent self-evolution, DEFEND breaks through the limitations of traditional static defense baselines and demonstrates exceptional defensive capabilities. The introduction of audit mechanism effectively prevents the risk of misevolution. Through AuditorAgent's auditing of the reliability and logical integrity of generated tools, DEFEND ensures the trustworthiness of the dynamic defense system. As a pioneering effort toward unification in the field of agent defense, this framework showcases the potential for achieving universal agent protection and may inspire the development of more broadly applicable agent protection systems.
