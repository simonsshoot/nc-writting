\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
In this section, we will present the results of DEFEND framework. We first demonstrate its overall protection performance across different datasets, covering a wide range of fields including finance, healthcare, applications, and the web. Next, we demonstrate DEFEND's evolution over existing tools and the effectiveness of its auditing mechanism. Finally, we present ablation experiments to illustrate the necessity of each component. We also conduct a case study in the AI2THOR simulation environment to demonstrate the real-world significance of DEFEND framework, shown in Appendix~\ref{appendix:case_study}. Cases of generated guardrail tools are in Appendix~\ref{appendix:toolcase} and cases of tool evolution are in Appendix~\ref{appendix:toolevolutioncase}.
\subsection{experimental Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}
\paragraph{Benchmarks.} 
To demonstrate the effectiveness of DEFEND framework, we conduct extensive experiments on three cutting-edge datasets. \ding{172} R-Judge~\citep{yuan2024rjudge} is a benchmark dataset that covers high-quality LLM agent interaction records in application, finance, IoT, program, and web scenarios, involving ten risk types. \ding{173} ASSEBench~\citep{luo2025agentauditor} is a large-scale, high-quality benchmark dataset that jointly covers both safety and security aspects of LLM agents, enabling a systematic evaluation of agent's defensive capabilities. \ding{174} AgentHarm~\citep{andriushchenko2025agentharm} is specifically designed to evaluate the security and robustness of LLM agents across multiple scenarios involving tool calls, based on 110 core malicious behaviors.
\paragraph{Evaluation Metrics.}
As the task can be viewed as binary classification task(whether the query and action are safe or not), we adopt four classification metrics (safe being the positive): Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and F1-score for evaluation. Accuracy measures the overall proportion of correctly identified operations; Precision reflects the reliability of the safety judgments; Recall measures the framework's ability to identify all truly safe operations; F1 is the harmonic mean of precision and recall, providing a balance on the trade-off between them.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We select representative baselines from different types of current agent defense frameworks for comparison to demonstrate the superiority of DEFEND. \ding{172} CoTSafe~\citep{yin2024safeagentbench}: a prompt-based framework evaluating agent's every step during tasks. \ding{173} ShieldAgent~\citep{zhang2024asb}: a model-based framework, the safety of LLM agents' behavior is evaluated using a security judgment model fine-tuned from 4000 agent interaction records. \ding{174} GuardAgent~\citep{xiang2024guardagent}: an agent-based framework, using a guardrail agent with knowledge-enabled reasoning to guard another agent. \ding{175} AGrail~\citep{luo2025agrail}: a lifelong guardrail framework, with scalable safety criteria, providing effective and adaptive safety detection.

\subsection{Main Results}
\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{code/main_result.pdf}
    \caption{Main results of the five frameworks on three benchmarks. Heatmap shows the performance of each framework across different metrics, with darker colors indicating better performance. DEFEND performs well, significantly outperforming the baseline across multiple metrics.}
    \label{fig:main_result}
\end{figure}
The experimental results are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:main_result}. We compare the performance of DEFEND with four baseline defense frameworks on four mainstream LLMs to fully demonstrate the superiority of the framework, settings are in Appendix~\ref{appendix:setting}.

DEFEND demonstrates overwhelming dominance across all test combinations. In 12 test scenarios, DEFEND nearly sweeps the top scores across all evaluation metrics. On R-Judge benchmark, DEFEND achieves excellent performance with over 90 percentage across all five metrics; on AssEBench, DEFEND outperforms the runner-up by almost 10 percentage on evaluation metric F1; and on AgentHarm, DEFEND significantly outperforms the second-place baseline across all four base LLMs and four comprehensive metrics. This comprehensive lead powerfully demonstrates the correctness of viewing defense mechanisms as ``evolvable and optimizable artifacts'', indicates that agents are not only tool users but also have the potential to become excellent tool creators. Furthermore, DEFEND exhibits excellent cross-model generalization. Whether base LLM is the open-source leader DeepSeek or the closed-source giants GPT-4o, Claude, and Gemini, DEFEND's defense effectiveness consistently remains at a very high level. Among them, DEFEND performs best on Gemini, but the difference is not significant, with F1 score remaining relatively stable between 0.86 and 0.92. Relying on its self-evolving adaptability, the guardrail tool can be ``tailored'' in time according to specific requests and operations, thereby compensating for the differences in native security awareness capabilities among different base LLMs and achieving truly universal security protection.

DEFEND also demonstrates excellent cross-benchmark and cross-domain generalization. As mentioned before, most traditional defense frameworks rely on hard-coded rules, fixed domains, or predefined tools. This static nature makes them difficult to freely promote across domains with varying security requirements. Experimental data strongly validates this viewpoint: existing solutions exhibit severe performance fluctuations in cross-benchmark testing. For example, ShieldAgent performs reasonably well on the R-Judge benchmark, achieving an F1 score of 0.83, but when applied to the AgentHarm benchmark, its performance drops sharply, with the F1 score plummeting to 0.18, indicating complete detection failure. GuardAgent also shows drastic fluctuations when migrating across the three benchmarks, with F1 scores varying by up to 20\% across different datasets. Defense mechanisms based on static rules or domain-specific constraints often lack sufficient adaptive capability when facing dynamically evolving tasks, resulting in significant limitations in generalization. In contrast, DEFEND, through continuous interaction with the environment and actively abstracting deep security patterns from interaction trajectories, maintains Accuracy scores above 0.87 when facing fundamentally different benchmarks. Through continuous optimization and self-summarization of guardrail tools, DEFEND transcends simple memorization of known threats and forms a transferable, universal response mechanism. The data proves that DEFEND successfully transforms abstract security policies into concrete code tools, demonstrating excellent protection performance and high portability across different tasks, domains, and environments.

DEFEND achieves a dynamic balance between precision and recall, marking a high degree of trustworthiness. Taking ShieldAgent as an example, while it attains a recall rate of 0.9934 on R-Judge, its precision remains mediocre across multiple benchmarks, indicating an aggressive strategy of blind interception. With its auditing mechanism, DEFEND questions the logical integrity of every newly evolved tool, effectively ensuring the rationality of each decision. Experimental results show that DEFEND maintains a high interception rate with almost no severe misjudgments. By transforming originally ambiguous natural language discrimination logic into verifiable and executable Python guardrail tool code, DEFEND provides a defense logic that is more reliable than traditional filtering mechanisms.

\subsection{Self-evolving Process}
\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{code/reuse_result.pdf}
    \caption{Results for DEFEND's evolving process on R-Judge benchmark. DEFEND can dynamically determine which tasks need security tools. Furthermore, DEFEND can continuously reuse and optimize existing tools to achieve self-evolution.}
    \label{fig:reuse_result}
\end{figure}
The experimental results are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:reuse_result}. We aim to demonstrate that DEFEND can dynamically analyze tool requirements and continuously generate new tools, reuse and optimize existing tools to achieve tool evolution.

DEFEND is capable of analyzing the necessity of employing guardrail tools, with data illustrating the proportion of guardrail tool invocations classified as needed versus not needed. Results show that over 50\% of tasks rely on guardrail tools generated by DEFEND to achieve effective protection. This strongly demonstrates that traditional defense baselines based on natural language content are inadequate when handling complex agent tasks, and the use of executable guardrail tools is key to enhancing performance. In experiments, DEFEND exhibits exceptional adaptive judgment, accurately identifying which requests require the invocation of security tools and which can be handled through simplified processes. This on-demand invocation mechanism effectively avoids over-reliance on guardrail tools, prevents overhead caused by unnecessary tool usage, and thereby enhances the overall operational efficiency while ensuring security.

The distribution between reused and new tools reveals DEFEND's self-evolution and resource optimization capabilities. By efficiently retrieving and reusing existing tools through FusionAgent, DEFEND conducts precise iteration and fusion based on current tools. On the Gemini base model, DEFEND achieves a tool reuse rate above 53\% across five domains. This high proportion of tool reuse significantly reduces the storage burden on the lifelong tool library and, through continuous optimization of existing tools, reflects the ongoing evolution of defense tools.

\subsection{Audit Mechanism}
\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{code/doubt_result.pdf}
    \caption{Results for DEFEND's auditing mechanism on R-Judge benchmark. Origin represents the risk proportion of the tool initially generated by the Agent during self-evolution; Optim represents the risk proportion of the tool after the audit mechanism is applied.}
    \label{fig:doubt_result}
\end{figure}
The experimental results are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:doubt_result}. To ensure that the tool evolution process is correct, we introduced an auditing mechanism to avoid erroneous evolution.

We discovere that even when safety constraints are prominently incorporated into the tool generation prompts, the tools initially evolved by the agent may still exhibit related security vulnerabilities. We term this phenomenon ``Vulnerabilities in Guardrail Tools (VGT)", detailed in Appendix~\ref{appendix:VGT}. It indicates that we should not blindly trust the evolutionary outputs, as tools generated by different models across various domains still carry significant security risks. Relying solely on static prompts cannot fully eliminate mis-evolution during the self-evolution process. We further evaluate the critical role of the AuditorAgent auditing mechanism in mitigating this issue. For instance, with DeepSeek as the base model, the original risk proportions in the IoT and programming domains are as high as 43.24\% and 44.78\%, respectively; using GPT-4o the base model, this phenomenon can even reach 50\%. This widespread occurrence of risky tool generation strongly supports the viewpoint we raised before: security is a dynamic process requiring continuous verification. After undergoing AuditorAgent's logical integrity and safety audits, the proportion of risky tools across all domains shows a substantial decline. Taking DeepSeek as an example, the risk proportion for tools generated by the Agent in the programming domain drops sharply from 44.78\% to 8.20\%. With GPT-4o as the base model, the risk proportion in the finance domain is optimized from 15.89\% to low 1.87\%. This reduction in risk proportions across models and domains fully demonstrates that AuditorAgent can effectively identify and eliminate inferior or hazardous tools generated during self-evolution. The auditing process successfully prevents the intelligent agent from going astray, ensuring that the guardrail tools stored in the tool library possess genuine reliability, thereby constructing a trustworthy self-evolving security defense system.

\subsection{Ablation Study}
\begin{wrapfigure}{r}{0.5\textwidth}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{code/ablation_study.pdf}
    \caption{Ablation study results on R-Judge benchmark. The performance of DEFEND drops after removing any of the three core components, demonstrating the necessity of each component.}
    \label{fig:ablation_result}
\end{wrapfigure}

The experimental results are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:ablation_result}. To demonstrate the necessity of DEFEND’s core components, we design three types of ablation experiments: \ding{172} Remove AnalysisAgent, using predefined risk categories for risk analysis without introducing new risk categories; \ding{173} Remove FusionAgent, where AnalysisAgent directly passes newly generated tools to ExecutorAgent for execution; \ding{174} Remove AuditorAgent, eliminating auditing of tools and aggregation of results for final decision-making, instead relying directly on tool execution outcomes for judgment.

After removing AnalysisAgent, DEFEND shows significant degradation across all four metrics, with precision dropping by 35\% and F1 decreasing by 27\%. This indicates that fixed risk categories prevent DEFEND from effectively generalizing to other domains, directly impacting the generation of relevant guardrail tools and subsequent decision-making based on tool results. The dynamic and adaptive analysis by AnalysisAgent is indispensable for DEFEND's excellent generalization capability. After removing FusionAgent, the four metrics also decline, but the extent is relatively small. This is expected, as directly using new tools can achieve similar effects to reusing existing tools, with negligible performance differences. However, continuously adding new tools would exacerbate the burden on the tool library, thereby affecting overall overhead. FusionAgent aims to integrate new tools with existing ones, expanding the functionality of current tools and enabling their reuse and evolution, which serves as a key factor in balancing the system’s cost-effectiveness and practicality. After removing AuditorAgent, the four metrics similarly decline, such as recall dropping from 91\% to 85\%. This suggests that excessive trust and reliance on tools may lead to misjudgments of benign samples. Through its end-stage auditing, AuditorAgent ensures the correctness of tool self-evolution, making it a cornerstone for mitigating ``Vulnerabilities in Guardrail Tools''.
