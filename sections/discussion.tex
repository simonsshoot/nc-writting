\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{From static guardrails to evolving defense.}
A central limitation of existing agent defense frameworks is that they implicitly treat security as a closed-set classification problem: risks are assumed to be enumerable in advance and mitigated through predefined rules, prompts, or detectors. However, real-world environments are fundamentally open-ended. Emerging threats are often long-tailed, compositional, and context-dependent, making it impossible to exhaustively specify all potential failure modes before deployment. As a result, static defenses inevitably encounter a structural generalization bottleneck under distribution shifts.

Human societies address this problem differently. Research in cultural evolution and organizational adaptation suggests that resilience does not arise from memorizing every historical risk, but from continuously transforming concrete experiences into transferable protective strategies~\citep{boyd1988culture,henrich2015secret,weick2011managing}. Rather than storing isolated cases, human systems accumulate abstract operational principles that can generalize across novel situations. For example, repeated encounters with diverse financial fraud schemes eventually evolve into higher-level institutional mechanisms such as multi-factor verification and risk auditing. This process enables limited experiences to produce broad protective capabilities under previously unseen conditions. From this perspective, the key challenge for agent safety is not merely improving detection accuracy, but enabling defense systems to accumulate reusable security abstractions over time. EVOLVE explores this direction by externalizing security knowledge into executable guardrail tools that can be reused, recombined, and iteratively refined across domains. In this sense, executable guardrail tools function not simply as task-specific defenses but as transferable security mechanisms capable of evolving alongside changing environments.

\textbf{The self-evolution paradox.}
A notable observation of this work is that a self-evolved tool may itself become a source of unsafe risk. Even when explicitly optimized for safety, newly evolved guardrail tools can still contain hidden vulnerabilities and unsafe risks, a phenomenon we term VGT. At its core, this reflects a broader structural tension in self-evolving systems: optimization processes naturally prioritize functional effectiveness, while latent safety properties are often underconstrained and difficult to directly optimize. As a result, mechanisms intended to improve security may simultaneously introduce new attack surfaces during evolution. This phenomenon is not unique to LLM agents. Similar patterns have long been observed in biological evolution, organizational governance, and software engineering, where systems optimized for short-term functionality frequently accumulate hidden fragilities over time~\citep{taleb2012things,weick2011managing,khoury2023secure}. In AI-generated software, for example, models often produce code that is functionally correct yet security-vulnerable because optimization signals primarily reward task completion rather than robustness guarantees~\cite{peng2025correct}. Our findings suggest that self-evolving agent systems may exhibit similar phenomenon, \ie, safety divergence during autonomous adaptation.

From this perspective, continuous verification becomes not merely an auxiliary safeguard, but a necessary counterpart to self-evolution itself. Evolution without independent auditing risks amplifies hidden vulnerabilities through iterative optimization and reuse. More broadly, these observations indicate that future self-improving intelligent systems may require governance mechanisms that co-evolve alongside capability growth, ensuring that adaptation remains bounded within verifiable safety constraints.


\textbf{Conclusion.} We present EVOLVE, a self-evolving defense framework for LLM agents that transforms security from static policy enforcement into a continuously adaptive capability. By integrating executable guardrail evolution with independent auditing, EVOLVE enables defense mechanisms to accumulate, refine, and verify security knowledge through lifelong interaction. Extensive experiments across benchmark and real-world environments demonstrate EVOLVE's strong robustness under different task scenarios. Our findings suggest that achieving scalable safety for future intelligent agents may require defense systems that can evolve alongside the environments in which they operate.
